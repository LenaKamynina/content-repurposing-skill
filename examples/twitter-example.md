# X / Twitter Examples

**Important structural note:** these are five real, standalone Mailchimp tweets — each is a single post (short copy + one image or video), not a connected multi-tweet thread. They do **not** demonstrate the thread structure in [platform-formats.md](../platform-formats.md) (hook tweet → 2–8 one-idea-per-tweet body → CTA/summary close). Use these only as **tone and phrasing anchors** — hook style, stat-citing, hashtag/mention use, link placement — not as a structural template. When writing the actual thread, follow the structure in `platform-formats.md`, not the single-post format shown here.

## Example 1: Stat-led hook, seasonal relevance

> Halloween is having a moment. In fact, the National Retail Federation expects Americans to spend $12.2B on Halloween this year alone.
>
> But you don't need to have a direct Halloween product or tie-in to tap into the revenue opportunities the holiday brings. Find out more 👇

Image headline: "Any brand can get in on spooky season" / "Halloween spending keeps growing. Here's how to tap into it." Linked source: nrf.com

## What these demonstrate (tone/phrasing only)

- **Hooks lead with a stat or a moment**, not a generic statement — "$12.2B," "#WorldMentalHealthDay," "last day of Marketing Success Season."
- **Hashtags are tied to a real occasion** (#WorldMentalHealthDay), never used as generic reach-bait.
- **Emoji used sparingly and purposefully** — the 👇 in Example 1 points at an actual next action, not decoration.
- **Partnerships and people are named specifically** (@Headspace, Rania Succar with her title, Ian Hammersley as "best-selling author," Katie Potochney with her role) — credibility through specificity, not vague "our team."
- **Positive, human framing** — "working happier and healthier," "making the world a better place" — never forced or over-the-top.
- **CTA/link sits at the end**, phrased as a direct next step (Find out more, Access them now, register here).
- **Active voice throughout**, consistent with brand-voice.md.
