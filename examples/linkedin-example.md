# LinkedIn Examples

Use these as format and tone anchors when drafting LinkedIn posts. Match the rhythm, hook style, and structure — not necessarily every word choice (see note on Example 1 below).

## Example 1

> Is your content feeling a little too perfect?
>
> In a world of AI-generated images and overly-polished ads, audiences crave what's real. In Chapter 7 of The Ecommerce Playbook, Cody Wittick shares why "shaky, raw, and native-to-the-feed" content is currently crushing it.
>
> Cody's how-to for young DTC brands working with creators includes:
> - Product seeding. Send your product out with no strings attached. You'll likely get UGC back.
> - Going micro. Forget the million-follower accounts. Working with micro-influencers (sub 150k) has tons of benefits for young DTC brands.
> - Keeping it raw. Don't over-edit! Upload creator content as-is to your ads.
> - Filling the gaps. Audit your content library and see what you're missing. Do you need more video? Static? Human-forward or product-forward?
>
> Read Cody's full take in Chapter 7 of the Playbook, link in the comments.

**Note:** this example uses "crushing it," which is on the words-to-avoid list in [brand-voice.md](../brand-voice.md). Treat the hook, structure, and bulleted how-to format as the pattern to follow — don't carry that specific phrase into new output.

**What this demonstrates:** question hook that names a real tension, a named source/expert for credibility, a scannable bulleted breakdown, single clear next step at the end.

Plus with Sidekick, Shopify's AI assistant, you can access Intuit Mailchimp performance insights and act on them — all without ever leaving your Shopify store.
