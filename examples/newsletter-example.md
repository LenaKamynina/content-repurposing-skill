# Newsletter Examples

**Important structural note:** these are real Mailchimp marketing emails — short promotional blocks (headline + 2–3 sentence body + single CTA button, often several such blocks per email). They do **not** match the "Newsletter Section" structure in [platform-formats.md](../platform-formats.md) (a single continuous 300–500 word section, context → insight → implication, max 1–2 links). Use these only as **tone and phrasing anchors** — headline style, sentence rhythm, CTA phrasing, how stats are cited — not as a structural template. When writing the newsletter section, follow the structure in `platform-formats.md`, not the block layout shown here.

## Example 1: SMS feature promotion

**Headline:** Open up new marketing pathways with SMS

**Body:** SMS helps you reach new places with alerts, promos, and more, going beyond email to transform how you engage customers. It's why Mailchimp SMS e-commerce users saw up to 20x ROI in the first 3 months after launching their first SMS marketing campaign. Add it on to your plan today.

**CTA:** Explore SMS

**Supporting block — "Find ways to boost your reach—and your ROI":**
- Connect with your customers in new ways with SMS's rapid audience responses and higher deliverability rates compared to email.
- Drive sales through fast, meaningful engagement by adding SMS to your customer journeys—and automate your communications in one place.
- Unify your customer profiles, journeys, and analytics across email and SMS...

## What these demonstrate (tone/phrasing only)

- Headlines pose a question or state a direct benefit ("What could you do with SMS this powerful?", "Unify your data to help campaigns convert").
- Body copy is tight — 2–3 sentences, one core benefit, occasionally a cited stat ("up to 20x ROI," "294% higher click rates").
- Em dash used correctly for asides ("You've come this far—let's take the next step forward together").
- CTAs are short verb phrases (Explore SMS, Upgrade now, View all updates) — not full sentences.
- Stats are specific and sourced (footnote markers in the originals), never vague ("a lot more revenue").
